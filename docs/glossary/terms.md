# Period Terms

Vocabulary that turns up in the game's UI, dialogue, kit descriptions,
and the Quartermaster's wares. Period-accurate to the Wars of the Three
Kingdoms (1638–1651) and the Earl of Loudoun's Regiment of Foote
specifically.

Entries are short on purpose — one or two sentences each. Cross-linked
terms are **bold**.

---

## A

### Advance Your Pike
A pike posture — held vertical, butt in the right hand, shaft tucked
into the shoulder. The standard marching-in-column posture. See
[drill-commands.md](drill-commands.md).

### Apostles (the twelve)
Twelve wooden powder containers slung from a **bandolier**, each holding
one pre-measured musket charge. So called because of the count, not
sanctity. Issued to musketeers as standard kit at **Trained Band** tier
and above.

## B

### Back-and-breast
A two-piece cuirass (back plate + breast plate) worn over a buff coat.
Marker of veteran or officer status. Heavy, hot, expensive — rare on
the Covenanter side, more common as battlefield loot from English
Royalists or New Model Army.

### Bandolier
A leather belt worn over the shoulder, holding the **apostles** plus a
priming flask and a bullet bag. Standard musketeer kit.

### Block
Either a formation of pikemen (the standing unit) or, casually, any
formation of foot. The game uses "block" for the in-game unit.

### Blue bonnet
The signature headgear of Covenanter foot. Identifies you instantly as
Scots Presbyterian-aligned. Worn from Tier 1 (**Trained Band**) onward.

### Bringer-up
The rear-rank man of a file — historically the next-most-experienced
soldier after the file leader, capable of holding the file together if
the front rank breaks.

### Buff coat
A thick, oiled leather coat worn under armour or in place of it. Cheap
musket-ball resistance, decent against blades. Tier 3 (**Veteran**) kit.

## C

### Caliver
A lighter musket variant, easier to handle without a rest. Less common
than the matchlock musket by the 1640s but still in service.

### Charge for Horse
Anti-cavalry pike posture — front two ranks ground their pike butts
behind a planted right foot and angle the points at horse-chest height.
Rear ranks **Charge Your Pike** over their heads. Often combined with
**Form Circle** when flanking pike blocks aren't available.

### Charge Your Pike
The fighting pike posture — pike lowered to chest height, butt swung
behind the body, left hand under the chin. Adopted by the front two
ranks of a block in combat; remainder hold **Port**.

### Closest Order
The tightest individual spacing — pikemen shoulder-touching. Maximum
push power, no room to manoeuvre. Used at the moment of impact in a
**Push of Pike**.

### Close Order
Shoulder-to-shoulder spacing, just enough room to face or step. The
standard pre-contact spacing.

### Corporal
NCO rank, typically marks the rear of a pike block (front of a musket
block). Maintains dressings, rallies stragglers during a **Reform**.

### Countermarch
A manoeuvre that reverses a block's facing by marching the front rank
through the formation to become the new rear. Comes in three flavours —
maintaining, losing, or gaining ground — depending on where the block
ends up.

### Covenanter
A Scots Presbyterian aligned with the National Covenant (1638) and the
Solemn League and Covenant (1643). Politically opposed to Charles I's
religious policy; militarily organised into the Covenanter Army.
Loudoun's Regiment is Covenanter.

## D

### Dressings
The continuous obligation to fill gaps in your rank and file. Step
forward if there's a hole in front of you; the block lives or dies on
its dressings.

### Drill
Practised manoeuvring of a block under verbal command. Period sources
(Markham, Barriffe) catalogue dozens of drill commands; the regiment's
working set is captured in [drill-commands.md](drill-commands.md).

## E

### Ensign
A junior officer rank — historically the bearer of the regimental
colours. An Ensign's presence on the field steadies a block; his loss
breaks it.

## F

### File
A vertical line of pikemen from front rank to rear. A 4×4 block has
four files of four men each. Contrast **rank** (horizontal line).

### File leader
The front man of a file — usually one of the more experienced soldiers,
since he sets the example and absorbs the first impact.

### Form Circle
A defensive formation — pikemen face outward in a ring, outer ring
adopts **Charge for Horse**, interior adopts **Charge Your Pike** to
cover gaps. Used to shield non-combatants (musketeers, camp followers)
from cavalry that can attack from any direction.

## H

### Halve Your Pike (Shorten Your Pike)
Grip the pike at its midpoint to halve its effective reach, for
close-quarters work. Called when the block is going in slow-and-tight
or at the run.

### High Port (Port)
A pike posture — pike held at 45° pointing skyward, left hand under the
chin. Used as a salute, as a transition to **Charge Your Pike**, and by
rear ranks of a Charged block (pikes pass over front ranks' heads).
Distinct from **Low Port**.

### Hodden grey
The undyed wool fabric of standard Covenanter coats. Cheap, practical,
slightly grey-brown. Tier 2 (**Regimented Foot**) kit.

## J

### Jeddart staff
A Scottish polearm — long-handled blade, used as a budget pike at
Tier 0 (**Levy**) when actual pikes weren't available.

## L

### Levy
Tier 0 kit — the "just turned out by the **wapinschaw**" look. Bonnet,
sark (linen shirt), trews or breeks, plaid, brogues. Maybe a dirk.
Pike, half-pike, or **Jeddart staff** for a weapon.

### Lobster-tail pot
A helmet with an articulated neck guard resembling a lobster's tail.
Officer / veteran cavalry kit; rare on Covenanter foot.

### Low Port
The pre-push pike posture — pike held across the body diagonally toward
the left shoulder, lower than High Port. Pikeman drops his height (lean
forward, bend the knees) to maximise sustained leg drive after impact.
Distinct from **High Port**.

## M

### Markham, Gervase
17th-century English military writer; one of the canonical sources for
pike drill. Period commands in [drill-commands.md](drill-commands.md)
trace back to his manuals (and others — Barriffe etc.).

### Montero cap
A soft, brimmed wool cap with ear flaps. Tier 2 (**Regimented Foot**)
headgear; cheaper alternative to a **pot helmet**.

### Musket
A heavy matchlock firearm requiring a rest in early-war use. Issued to
musketeers from Tier 1 (**Trained Band**) onward.

## N

### New Model Army
The professionalised Parliamentary force formed in 1645. Better-paid,
better-equipped, standardised kit. Loot from engagements with the New
Model emphasises reliable but unflashy gear (pot helmets,
back-and-breasts).

## O

### Order
The standard parade-ground spacing — roughly three feet between
individuals in all directions. The block's default distance unless
ordered otherwise.

### Order Your Pike
A pike posture — butt grounded outside the right foot, right hand
gripping the shaft at chin height. The resting / parade-ground
default.

## P

### Pike
A long thrusting spear, typically 14–18 ft, used by infantry against
cavalry and other infantry. The defining weapon of the regiment.

### Plunder
The irregular, loot-derived half of the campaign's dual currency. Buys
flashier but inconsistent gear from quartermasters. Earned by defeating
enemy blocks.

### Pay
The regular but sparse half of the dual currency. Reflects period
reality — Covenanter pay was famously unreliable. Buys baseline kit
from quartermasters.

### Pot helmet (the "iron hat")
The standard Scots Covenanter helmet at Tier 3. Simple iron skullcap
with a brim. The same general design served from **Trained Band**
through **Veteran**, with quality varying.

### Push of Pike
The decisive close-combat resolution where two pike blocks lock their
pikes and physically push against each other until one block breaks.
A test of formation, discipline, leg drive, and nerve.

## Q

### Quartermaster
The NPC vendor of gear. Different regions stock different inventories
— Edinburgh QM trades muskets and coats; a Highland muster QM trades
dirks, targes, and Lochaber axes. Famously grumbling.

## R

### Rank
A horizontal line of pikemen from one side of the block to the other,
all facing the same direction. A 4×4 block has four ranks of four men.
Contrast **file** (vertical line).

### Reform
The procedure for reconstituting a block after disorder. Pikes form up
at **Close Order** in **Advance Your Pike**; Ensign marks the area;
Corporal marks the front-left. Muskets form up at **Order** in **Order
Your Musket**; Corporal marks the front-right.

## S

### Sark
A linen shirt worn under everything else. Tier 0 (**Levy**) and above.

### Snapsack
A simple cylindrical canvas bag closed at both ends with a strap.
Period equivalent of a backpack. Issued from **Trained Band** onward.

## T

### Targe
A round wooden shield with a leather face, often spiked. Highland kit
— available from a Highland muster Quartermaster but not from regular
Lowland supply.

### Trail Your Pike
A low-profile carrying posture — pike held in the right hand near the
head, butt dragged behind. Used only in dense woodland or for passing
under low archways. Period sources call it "contemptible" because it
slows reaction time.

### Trained Band
Tier 1 kit. The minimum-credible Covenanter pikeman: **blue bonnet**,
wool doublet, hose, proper shoes, leather belt, **snapsack**. Issued
a full pike or matchlock musket.

## V

### Veteran
Tier 3 kit. **Pot helmet** ("iron hat"), **buff coat**, bandolier with
quality flask, sword + dirk. Possibly captured English kit — a nice
flavour reason for it being on a Scotsman.

## W

### Wapinschaw (or Wappenschawing)
The Scots militia muster — a periodic gathering where the laird's men
turned out with whatever weapons they had. Source of Tier 0 (**Levy**)
"just shown up" recruits.

### Wheeling
A movement command — the block rotates around a pivot point (one end
or the middle) while marching, to change direction without halting.
The inside file barely moves while the outside file traces the widest
arc.
