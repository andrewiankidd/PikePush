# Drill Commands

The pike-drill commands as taught and practised by The Earl of Loudoun's
Regiment of Foote. Each entry describes what the pikeman physically does
when the command is given, and when it's used.

For broader period vocabulary, see [terms.md](terms.md).

---

## Pike postures

Postures describe **how the pike is held**. They are orthogonal to
formation and spacing — a block can be in any posture at any spacing.
Most postures matter in combat: charge postures push, defensive postures
resist cavalry, low postures prepare for impact.

### Order Your Pike
Rest posture. Butt grounded outside the right foot, right foot slightly
forward of the left. Right hand grips the shaft around chin height.
Left hand sits behind the back at waist height, fist loosely closed
(simulating the hilt of the side-arm pikemen historically carried).
The parade-ground default.

### Advance Your Pike
Marching posture. Pike vertical, butt cradled in the right hand, shaft
tucked into the right shoulder for support; left hand behind the back.
Verticality matters — close-quarter manoeuvring depends on each
pikeman keeping the shaft out of his neighbours' way. New recruits and
veterans-in-high-winds may grip the shaft at shoulder level with the
left hand for stability.

### Shoulder Your Pike
Long-march posture. Pike rests over the right shoulder; the butt swings
forward roughly six feet ahead of the pikeman, held six inches off the
ground. Right hand grips with forearm parallel to the ground; left hand
behind the back. Faster tempo than Advance. *Don't bounce the butt — the
pikeman behind you is in the strike zone.*

### Charge Your Pike
Combat posture. Right hand stays behind the butt (never let it slip —
the man behind is at risk) and swings the butt rearward, twisting the
torso. Left hand locks under the chin with the forearm vertical and the
elbow tucked into the waist. Left leg slightly bent, right leg braced.
Pikes lower to the right of the man in front. **Rank-specific:** only
the front two ranks adopt Charge — the rest hold Port over their heads.

### Charge to the Rear
Reverses the block's facing while preserving the Charge posture. A
scripted sequence: Advance Your Pike → Left Hand About Face → Charge
Your Pike → re-dress. Used when the threat is behind.

### Port (or High Port)
Pike held at 45° pointing skyward. Left hand under the chin as in
Charge, butt swung behind. Used as a salute, as the transitional
posture before Charge, for passing through gateways, and by the rear
ranks of a Charged block (where the pikes pass over the front ranks'
heads). **Distinct from Low Port below.**

### Low Port Your Pike
Push posture. Adopted immediately before contact with another block.
Drop your height: lean forward, bend the knees, get tight with your
neighbours. Butt in the right hand, shaft sloping diagonally across
the body toward the left shoulder, elbows down. The push isn't won at
impact — it's won by sustained leg drive *after* the lock-up, which
only works if you started low.

### Shorten Your Pike (a.k.a. Halve Your Pike)
Grip the shaft near the midpoint, balanced, held at waist height.
Halves the effective reach for close-quarters fighting. Called when the
block is going in slow-and-tight or at a run; pikemen fight in pairs
where possible. Watch the men behind when shifting side-to-side or
stabbing.

### Charge for Horse
Anti-cavalry posture. Drive the pike butt into the ground; right foot
behind it as a backstop so it won't slip when a horse hits the head.
Step forward with the left leg into a crouch; left arm rests on the
left knee with the left hand aiming the pike head at the horse's
chest. **Rank-specific:** front two ranks only — rear ranks Charge
their pikes over the kneelers' heads. Normally only used when the
block has friendly pike blocks on both flanks (which cover the sides).
Otherwise see Form Circle.

### Form Circle
Hedgehog formation, used to shield non-combatants (musketeers, camp
followers) from cavalry that can come from any direction. The outer
ring of pikemen face outward, step in toward the centre to tighten the
perimeter, ground their pike butts, and adopt the Charge for Horse
posture. Pikemen in the interior of the circle go to Charge Your Pike
to cover gaps between the outward-facing ranks. Bring the points fully
down only after the unarmed elements are inside.

### Trail Your Pike
A sloppy carrying posture, used only when terrain demands — dense
woodland, very low archways. Pike held in the right hand about 18
inches behind the head, butt dragged on the right side. Period sources
describe it bluntly as a "contemptible posture" because it slows
reaction time. Included for completeness.

## Distancing

Default convention: distance changes happen **from the right and front**
unless overridden (with "from the Left", "from the Midst", or "from the
Rear"). Distances can apply to ranks only, files only, or both.

| Spacing | Distance between individuals | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Closest Order | shoulder-touching | Maximum push power. Frightening for new recruits. |
| Close Order | shoulder-to-shoulder | Just enough room to face or move; can go to Closest at the last moment. Also for narrow gaps. |
| Order | ~3 ft | Standard parade-ground distance. |
| Open Order | ~6 ft | Required before doubling, countermarching, or interpenetration. |
| Double Distance | ~12 ft | Rare. |
| Twice Double Distance | ~24 ft | Almost never used; here for completeness. |

### Directional opening variants

These specify *who holds station* during the opening, which determines
where the block ends up in space afterward.

- **Bring Your Files to Open Order** — Senior file leader and his file
  hold; everyone else moves sideways until 6 ft separates each pikeman.
  Block widens, depth unchanged. Used to interpenetrate with another
  block, prepare for doubling, or set up a countermarch.
- **Bring Your Files to Open Order from the Left** — Senior file
  leader still holds, but the opening propagates leftward; the leftmost
  file moves furthest.
- **Bring Your Files to Open Order from the Midst** — Symmetric
  opening from the centre (the imaginary file between the 2nd and 3rd
  file leaders). Centre holds; sides spread outward.
- **Bring Your Ranks to Open Order** — Depth increases without
  widening. Front rank holds; following ranks step back to 6 ft
  separation.
- **Bring Your Ranks to Open Order from the Rear** — The Corporal's
  rank (rearmost) holds; everyone else moves forward.
- **Ranks and Files to Open Order** — Both axes simultaneously. Senior
  file leader holds; everyone else opens to 6 ft in every direction.
  "From the Right" or "from the Midst" qualifiers apply as above.

## Facings

A facing pivots the block in place. Left Hand About Face is the
canonical 180° turn — it's the same rotation that begins a countermarch.

- **Right Hand Face / Left Hand Face** — 90° pivot in the named
  direction.
- **Left Hand About Face** — 180° pivot leftward. Used as-is or as a
  countermarch preparative.
- **Face to the Front** — Period command for "return to original
  facing" — the direction the file leaders originally pointed.
- **Right Hand Incline / Left Hand Incline** — Performed **while
  marching**, not from a halt. The block continues forward but drifts
  gradually to the named side. Used to take ground not directly ahead,
  or to skirt obstacles without halting.
- **Face to the Front and Rear** — Defensive split. Front ranks hold
  their facing; from the half-file leaders rearward, everyone Left Hand
  About Faces. The block defends front and rear simultaneously.
- **Face to Both Flanks** — Defensive split sideways. Right-side files
  Right Hand Face, left-side files Left Hand Face. The "we're
  surrounded and breaking out" posture.

## Dressings

Not a command — a continuous obligation. The standing rule: **if there
is a gap in front of you, step forward and fill it.**

Dressings matter most after any movement that disrupts ranks/files —
most notably after a Left Hand About Face when files have uneven
lengths (common when musketeers are concentrated on one flank). The
new front rank may have gaps where short files used to be; pikemen in
those files step forward to even the new frontage.

## Doublings

Doublings re-arrange the formation to put more men in the front rank,
trading depth for frontage. Three methods exist, each with a different
trade-off:

| Method | Speed | Flexibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| By Half Files | slower | high | Rear half-files can break off as a separate block. |
| By Ranks | fast | low | Doesn't need designated half-file leaders. Most common in combat. |
| By Bringers Up | slow | low | Brings the rear (next-most-experienced) rank to the front. Mostly ceremonial today. |

All require correct spacing and dressings before the order is given.

### By Half Files

- **Half Files to the Left Double** — The half-file leader and his
  rear half-file march left to rank alongside their original file
  leader; they end up forming the left side of the new (wider) front
  rank.
- **Half Files to the Right Double** — Mirror, rightward.
- **Half Files Recover** — Reverses the doubling.

### By the Entire (rear half-files separate as their own block)

The signal phrase is "By the Entire". Instead of merging into the
existing front rank, the rear half-files form a *separate* block. The
sudden appearance of a fresh musket-block on a flank is the appeal —
it's a morale weapon as much as a tactical one.

- **By the Entire, Half Files to the Left Double** — Rear half forms a
  separate block on the left.
- **By the Entire, Half Files to the Right Double** — Mirror.
- **By the Entire, Half Files to the Outwards Double** — Rear half
  splits, taking both flanks. Two new sub-blocks appear, one on each
  side of the original front rank.
- **Half Files Recover** — Reverses.

### By Ranks

The fast method. Even-numbered ranks step right (or left) and forward
to interleave between the odd ranks, halving rank count and doubling
frontage. No leader designations required.

- **Ranks to Right Double**
- **Ranks to Left Double**
- **Ranks Recover** — Reverses.

### By Bringers Up

- **Bringers Up, Double the Frontage to the Left Hand** — Rear rank
  marches through the block to the left of the front rank; following
  ranks file in behind.
- **Bringers Up, Double the Frontage to the Right Hand** — Mirror.
- **Bringers Up Recover** — Reverses; half-file leaders move first.

### Combined

- **By the Entire** — Applies the most recent doubling to the whole
  block (involves combined about-facing and wheeling). Period sources
  describe it as advanced.

## Inversion / Filing On

Reordering the block into a column (or back) to pass through narrow
obstacles — gates, paths, doorways. The reform command **"Recover the
Body"** restores normal formation once the obstacle is cleared.

- **Files, File On** — Single-file column. Senior file leader leads;
  his file's last man is followed by the second file leader's whole
  file, and so on. Corporal brings up the rear.
- **Double Files, File On** — Two-file column. First two file leaders
  lead side-by-side; following files trail in pairs. Faster recovery
  but needs a wider gap.
- **Ranks, from the Left File On** — Front rank marches off first as
  a single file; senior file leader leads; his second-rank-man falls
  in behind the second file leader, and so on.
- **Ranks, from the Right File On** — Mirror.
- **Files to the Left Double** — Two-file consolidation. Senior file
  leader and the fourth file stand; the second and third files step
  back to form the second column. Reforms a fighting front quickly —
  preferred over Ranks File On when combat is imminent.

## Countermarching

A countermarch reverses the block's facing *through* itself — the front
rank physically moves through the block to become the new rear, instead
of just rotating in place. Three variants differ in where the block
ends up afterward.

Each is a two-part order: **Prepare to Countermarch [variant]** first,
then the execution word **Countermarch** when the officer is ready.
Correct spacing and dressings beforehand are critical — collisions are
the failure mode.

- **Prepare to Countermarch Maintaining Ground** — Block ends in the
  same spot, facing reversed. On *Countermarch*, the front rank Left
  Hand About Faces and marches back through the formation; each man
  halts where his file leader halted.

- **Prepare to Countermarch Losing Ground** — Block ends *behind* its
  starting position. Starts stationary; can continue into march. On
  *Countermarch*, the front rank Left Hand About Faces and marches
  rearward; when their file leader passes the second man, that second
  man also Left Hand About Faces and follows. Ranks don't move until
  the man in front of them has passed through. The block effectively
  retreats while reversing.

- **Prepare to Countermarch Gaining Ground** — Block ends *forward*
  of its starting position; ends stationary. On *Countermarch*, the
  front rank Left Hand About Faces, marches rearward, then halts; the
  rest of the block marches forward past them, each man halting and
  Left Hand About Facing once level with the now-rear rank's file
  leader.

## Wheeling

A wheel rotates the block around a pivot while it's moving. Unlike a
Face (in-place pivot), a wheel can only happen on the march. The
inside file barely moves while the outside file traces the widest arc.
The block continues wheeling until **March On** resumes straight-line
movement.

- **Right Hand Wheel** — Pivot on the second file leader (the
  rightmost-but-one — the actual inside file). Leftmost file walks
  the longest arc.
- **Left Hand Wheel** — Pivot on the senior file leader. Rightmost
  file walks the longest arc.
- **Wheel about the Midst to the Right Hand** — Pivot at the centre
  of the block (between the middle file leaders). Right-side files
  step backward, left-side files step forward. Same patch of ground;
  block ends rotated. Hard to execute cleanly — done slowly and
  carefully it's striking.
- **Wheel about the Midst to the Left Hand** — Mirror.

## Reforms

Not a command but a procedure — how the block reconstitutes itself
after disorder (post-fight, post-rout, post-dispersal).

### Pike block reform

1. Form up at **Close Order** in **Advance Your Pike** posture.
2. If an Ensign is fielded, head for him — he marks the general area.
3. The Corporal marks the **front-left** of the block.
4. From here the block can go to Closest Order to push, drop to Order
   to manoeuvre, or step up to Charge Your Pike.

### Musket block reform

Similar pattern, but: form up at **Order Your Musket** at **Order**
distance, and the Corporal marks the **front-right** instead.

### Rules of thumb

- Don't assume what comes next. Listen to orders.
- Never run through the front rank.
- If men are missing from your file or rank, dress forward — close
  the gap.
- If injured and unable to retake your place, signal someone. Don't
  just vanish from the formation.

## Movement

- **Halt** — Stop marching.
- **Forward March** — Begin marching forward at march tempo.

## Canonical naming reminders

A few naming distinctions worth keeping straight — the period sources
are precise about these:

- **Charge Your Pike**, **Charge for Horse**, and **Low Port** are
  three different combat-related postures, used against different
  threats. Don't conflate.
- **Port** (= **High Port**) points up at 45°. **Low Port** is across
  the body, lower and tighter. Two different commands.
- **Left Hand About Face** is the canonical 180° turn.
- **Face to the Front** is the canonical "return to original facing"
  command — not strictly an About Face, even when it produces the
  same rotation from the current state.
